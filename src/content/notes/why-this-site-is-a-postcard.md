Most personal sites open with a headline, a grid of cards and a contact button. They work. They also look like every other site, and I did not want a place on the internet that felt like a template with my name in it.

So I started with a different question: what does it feel like to receive something from a person? A postcard does. It has a stamp, a postmark and a handwritten address. It says who it is from, where it was sent and when. That felt right for a personal site, so everything here follows from that one idea.

This note is the long version of why the site looks and behaves the way it does. It is about the look and the feel, not the code.

## The idea: a letter from me to you

The home page is the cover of an airmail letter. The sender is me, top left and big. The recipient is whoever happens to be reading, written on a ruled address line: "You, kindly reading". The stamp and the postmark sit top right, where they would on a real envelope.

I tried a few versions of this. The first repeated my name and my city three times and wasted a third of the screen. So now every fact appears once. My name is the headline. What I do sits under it. The postmark carries the place, the date and the live time in Bengaluru. Nothing is said twice.

The same thinking runs through the rest of the site. Notes are postcards. Dates are stamps. Even the contact option is a postbox.

## The palette: three colours only

The whole site is built from three colours:

- Soft White for the paper
- Sage Green for fills and small marks
- Deep Olive for ink

That is it. No grey scale, no extra accent. Everything else is a mix of those three, so the page always feels like it was printed with one set of inks.

Light is the default and it ignores your system setting on purpose. When I first built it, the OS preference showed the dark version, where green takes over and the cream paper disappears. I wanted the cream to lead. Dark mode is still there, but you switch to it yourself.

## Imperfect on purpose

The part I care about most is that nothing is perfectly clean. Real printed things are not.

- **The background** is a hand-dabbed dot grid. The dots are uneven in size, position and strength, and a few are missing. It stays very faint so it never competes with the words.
- **Every straight line** on the site is drawn, not ruled. Row separators, the line under the nav, bullets and the dividers on this very card are small wobbly pen strokes. The separators between notes are soft hand-struck dashes, like perforation.
- **The envelope border** has uneven stripe widths, ragged edges and a little missing ink, the way a hand-printed airmail border would.
- **The postmark** looks like a rubber stamp that was pressed slightly crooked. The ink is patchy, there is a faint doubled impression and a few stray specks. It is tilted about seven degrees.

I can feel the difference even if I cannot always point to it. Perfect lines look like software. Slightly off lines look like someone made them.

## The stamp and the portrait

The stamp in the corner is an illustrated portrait of me. My first attempt copied a single photo and it did not look like me, so I rebuilt it from front-facing photos instead: side-swept hair, round glasses, a light moustache and a small goatee. It is a likeness, not a trace.

The stamp has perforated edges and a dark band at the bottom for the caption. In dark mode the stamp changes colour with the page, so the postmark ink stays readable where it crosses it.

Dates follow the same language. In the notes list and on the About page, every year or date is a small perforated stamp. A range like 2022 to now has a line between the two years, and "now" has a dot that pulses softly because the story is still running.

## Small things that move

Micro interactions were a core part of the brief from the start. I wanted the site to reward curiosity without getting in the way.

- **The nav underline** draws itself with a hand-drawn stroke when you hover.
- **The stamp** can be pressed. Click anywhere on the hero and a rubber stamp lifts, slams down and leaves a round seal in ink. Each seal carries a different traditional blessing, like fortune, happiness or good luck, with a kanji in a double ring and the English underneath.
- **The weather note** on the hero is a tiny hand-drawn icon that matches the sky over Bengaluru right now. The sun's rays turn, rain falls, the moon bobs and the clouds drift.
- **The postmark** has a colon that blinks once a second so you know the clock is live.

All of it stops when your system asks for reduced motion.

## The lantern and the speaker

The light and dark switch is a lantern. It is drawn from the Hoi An lanterns on my recent trip: a scalloped cap, silk ribs and a tassel. In light mode it is lit, with a soft glow that spreads across the nav. It hangs from the top of the page and swings like a pendulum. Hover and it gets a push.

Switching the light off does not just flip the colours. The change spreads out from the lantern like light, and when you turn it off the light shrinks back into it.

Next to it is a hand-drawn speaker, because sound is part of the site. It is off by default. If you turn it on, small sounds are made in your browser, with no audio files: a tick on links, a soft switch sound on toggles, a thunk when a stamp lands and a paper rustle when you hover a note. They are quiet and slightly different each time. I have not yet listened to the full set with fresh ears, so the volume is still something to tune.

## The postbox

In the bottom right corner of every page there is a round Japanese pillar postbox, with the 〒 mark on its plaque and a little grass swaying at the base. Hover it and the hood lifts. A message bubble pops out like a letter picked from the box. Click it and you land in a WhatsApp chat with me, with a message already written.

Paper planes also fly out of its hatch and hit random spots on the page. That one has no practical use. It is just there because a postbox that only works is less fun than one that plays.

## The Japanese thread

The navigation has small Japanese sublabels under each word, for example メモ under Notes. The hero says 航空便, which means airmail. Wish seals use kanji. I love the restraint of Japanese design: lots of space, small precise marks and quiet details that you notice on the second look.

These labels are standard words and I have not had a native reader check them yet. If you read Japanese and spot something off, tell me.

## What the site is for

It is a home for a few things:

- **Work**, the products I design and build in enterprise software and AI
- **Notes**, which are short and quick, not essays. Something I tried, learned or noticed
- **Community**, the work I do with Friends of Figma Bangalore
- **Photography**, selected shots
- **Stuff**, the tools and gear I actually use, in the spirit of the Stuff pages I like
- **About**, where I came from and how I work

Some of these are still empty. Work, Community and Photography are marked coming soon, and I would rather say so than fill them with fake content.

## What I borrowed and what is mine

I looked at hvpandya.com for how notes and a Stuff page can feel, and at charmiekapoor.com for a personal site with real personality. Both showed me that a portfolio can have a point of view. The postcard, the stamps, the lantern and the postbox are my own take on that.

## Still undecided

Honesty is part of the idea too, so here is what is not settled:

- The fonts are placeholders. The site uses one family for text, metadata and labels, and I have not chosen the final pairing. I want to compare real options side by side before committing.
- A lot of the content is still placeholder, including this note.
- The site is not live yet. It will be once the real content is ready.

If something here made you smile, that was the plan.
